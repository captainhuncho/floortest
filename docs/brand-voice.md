# FloorTest voice guidelines

Sources: the pre-seed deck (Sept 2026), the home page, and the in-app and email copy as of 27 Sep 2026. Use this for every page, email, invite and post.

## Who we're talking to

| Audience | What they want | What they fear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Receivers**: DJs, curators, labels who get unreleased music every week | Hear what's new quickly, find the good ones for Friday, stop digging through DMs | Losing a great track, spending hours on admin, being nagged by senders |
| **Senders**: producers, artists, label reps | Know the track arrived and whether anyone heard it | Shouting into a void, their unreleased music leaking |

Receivers are the customer. Senders are the growth loop: every sender who has a good experience may become a receiver.

## The one-line idea

**The receiving desk for new music.** Use this as the category line everywhere. The hero line, *From the studio / to the floor.*, is the promise.

## Voice: four traits

1. **Booth-level plain.** Short words, short sentences. Say what happens, the way a DJ would say it to another DJ. *"Play, keep, next."* Not *"streamline your A&R workflow."*
2. **Honest about signals.** We never let a signal claim more than it means. "Listened" means 30 seconds of real playback, and we say so. This honesty is the product's edge, so it must be the brand's edge too.
3. **Calm, not hype.** No exclamation marks, no "revolutionise", no "game-changer". Confidence comes from specifics: *250 MB, WAV or MP3, 90 days.*
4. **On both sides of the desk.** Respect the sender even when writing to the receiver. We never make fun of bad demos or tell receivers to ignore people.

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Direct | Blunt or cold |
| Specific | Technical for its own sake |
| Warm to artists | Sentimental ("your art matters") |
| Quietly confident | Salesy or hyped |
| Scene-literate (booth, set, callout, crate) | Slang-heavy or trying too hard |

## Vocabulary (use these words, and only these)

The biggest current problem is that the same thing has several names. Pick one and stick to it.

| Use | Don't use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **your link** / **receiving link** (first mention) | receiver link, submission link, permanent receiver link, demo submission link | "Permanent" is a benefit to state, not part of the name: *"Your link never changes."* |
| **desk** | receiving room, workspace, inbox | The receiver's side of FloorTest. "Open your desk." |
| **track** | song, submission (in user-facing copy), demo (as a noun for every file) | Plans currently say "active songs". Change to "active tracks". Say "track", not "demo": promos and released music arrive too. "Demo" only for a specific unfinished track. Category line is "for new music" (decided 12 Oct 2026: released tracks arrive too; the core is exclusives and works in progress tested on the floor). |
| **callout** / **collection** | folder, project, campaign | A callout is the ask ("send me Afro house for Villagers"). A collection is where those tracks land. |
| **receiver** / **sender** | curator/artist as role names, user, submitter | In marketing, prefer the real people: "DJs, curators and labels" / "artists and producers". |
| **Keep** / **Not for me** | pass, reject, decline, no | Always capitalised as buttons, lowercase in running text: "tracks you keep". |
| **Delivered / Listened / Downloaded / Replied / Played out** | heard, used, opened, "spun" | These are defined signals. Never paraphrase them. **Played out** means the DJ marked it as played in a set: the floor test, and the payoff signal for senders (lead with it when it has happened; never imply it otherwise). |
| **private receipt** | tracking page, status page | |
| **FloorTest** | Floortest, Floor Test, FT | |

## Grammar and style

- **Sentence case** for headings and buttons. Buttons are verbs: *Claim your link*, *Open your desk*, *Send track*.
- **One case per CTA style.** Today the home page mixes "Claim your receiving link", "claim your permanent link" (lowercase) and "CLAIM" in uppercase CSS. Pick sentence case in source and let CSS do any uppercase styling.
- **Arrows:** `→` on primary CTAs only. `↗` only for links that open elsewhere.
- **Numbers as digits:** 20 tracks, 90 days, 30 seconds, 250 MB.
- **British spelling** (the company and data are UK-based): recognised, organise, colour. Currency stays in USD while pricing is in USD.
- **"You" is the reader.** "We" is FloorTest, used sparingly. Never "users".
- **No em-dash sign-offs pretending to be a person.** Emails sign off `— FloorTest`. Replies from receivers sign off with their own name.
- **Colons, not dashes,** to explain: *"No streaming links: the receiver gets the actual file."*

## Say it like this

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| The file went to the booth | The original was downloaded |
| Your music network | (cut: we don't have a network feature) |
| Make every private track easier to receive | Every track in one place. Ready to play. |
| Receiving should be easy. | Free for your first 20 tracks. |
| Create receiver | Create my link |
| DJ / curator name | Your name or alias |
| Hear back the moment your track moves | Get an email when it's listened to, downloaded or replied to |

## Channel notes

- **Site:** big claims only if a number backs them. Every section should answer "what happens to my track?"
- **Transactional email:** subject = the signal + the track. Body: one fact, one link, one line that explains what the signal does and doesn't mean. Plain text is on-brand; keep it.
- **Invites (receiver to receiver):** sound like a DJ passing on a tip, not a referral scheme.
- **Social:** show the product doing one thing (a 10-second clip of Keep → next track beats any slogan). Scene words are welcome; hashtags max two.

## Quick check before anything ships

1. Does every signal mean exactly what `src/lib/plans.ts` says it means?
2. Is each thing named with the word from the vocabulary table?
3. Could a DJ read it aloud in a loud room and be understood?
4. Is there a number where there could be a number?
5. Would the sender be fine reading what we wrote to the receiver?
