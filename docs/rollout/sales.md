## 1. Who buys Team

**1.1 Independent label (3 to 8 people)**
- Pain: tracks arrive in the A&R inbox, DMs and WeTransfer links that expire. Nobody knows who heard what. Replies depend on who's having a good week.
- Answers: one link with a callout brief · A&R / Admin and members, with activity per person · replies from members held for approval.
- Pitch: "One link for your label. Everyone listens, one voice replies."

**1.2 Radio show or station**
- Pain: presenters and producers get the same promos separately. Nobody can say whether a track was played on air.
- Answers: shared desk with Review together (comments, one shortlist) · Prepare for set (named folder, M3U8, Rekordbox XML) · Played out and Downloaded on the sender's receipt.
- Pitch: "Your station's tracks in one queue, ready for the show."

**1.3 DJ crew or collective**
- Pain: residents forward tracks to each other in group chats. Files get lost, and the same producer gets three different answers.
- Answers: one desk, everyone with their own login · shared shortlist (★ Shortlisted by …) · one sender-facing name ("heard by <crew>'s team").
- Pitch: "Bring your crew. One desk, one shortlist, one answer."

**1.4 Booking or management agency**
- Pain: tracks for several artists come in through one inbox. Assistants filter them with no record of who passed what on.
- Answers: a collection per callout or artist ask · roles (members review, owner/A&R make the call) · per-person activity.
- Pitch: "Sort the tracks for your roster without losing who did what."

## 2. Outreach sequence (labels and radio)

Signed by the owner, plain text, one link. Stop as soon as they answer.

1. **Subject: Where your label's tracks land**
   You get tracks through email, DMs and transfer links. FloorTest gives you one link, a shared desk for your team, and an honest receipt for every sender.
2. **Subject: Who heard it, and who replied**
   Each person on your desk has their own login, and activity shows who did what. Replies from members can wait for your approval before they go.
3. **Subject: Callouts with a brief**
   Ask for what you want: BPM, genres, deadline, a note. Senders see the brief before they upload, so you get fewer tracks that miss.
4. **Subject: 10 minutes, on a real desk**
   I can show you a desk with real tracks: review, keep, prepare a crate, a sender's receipt. Pick a time: {link}.
5. **Subject: Closing the loop**
   I won't email again. If timing changes, Free covers 20 tracks and Team is $19.99 a month for 5 people. {link}

## 3. Ten-minute demo (real desk, sample tracks)

Before you start: a demo desk with 8 to 10 sample tracks from consenting producers (or our own), one open callout, two teammates, one held reply.

1. **0:00 Your link (1 min).** Open `floortest.app/<handle>` as a sender. Show the brief, the file-first form and "What happens to the file".
2. **1:00 Send one track live (1 min).** Upload one track from your phone. It shows under Up next on the Floor within about 10 seconds.
3. **2:00 The Floor (1 min).** The waiting count, the 5-day line, Your desk (approvals, seats).
4. **3:00 Review Queue (2 min).** Play, K to keep, X for not for me, 1–4 to jump through the track. Oldest first. Batch-select three tracks.
5. **5:00 Review together (1 min).** A teammate's comment, the shared shortlist.
6. **6:00 Reply approval (1 min).** A member's held reply: edit, send. The decision stays private.
7. **7:00 Prepare for set (1 min).** Download kept: named folder, M3U8, Rekordbox XML. Open it in Rekordbox.
8. **8:00 The sender's receipt (1 min).** Show Listened (30 seconds of real playback), Downloaded and Replied, and say what each one does and doesn't mean.
9. **9:00 Plans and the next step (1 min).** Team, seats and the beta offer. Book the setup: claim the handle, invite the team.

**Objections**
1. *"Email works fine."* It works until a link expires or someone leaves the team. Here every track, decision and reply is on one desk.
2. *"$19.99 is a lot for a small label."* It covers 5 people, $4 each. Start on Free and move up when the second person joins.
3. *"Senders won't use another tool."* They don't need an account: an email code, a drop box, and a receipt they can follow.
4. *"What about leaks?"* Originals stay private and never get published. Nothing is shared without the sender's permission. Files are scanned on our own servers (live at launch; a launch gate).
5. *"Can I see who on my team actually listened?"* Yes. Activity shows who did each thing. Senders only see your account name.

## 5. Partnerships

1. **DJ schools and academies.** They get Free desks for students and a callout they can run as a course brief. We get receivers who learn the habit early. Offer: Pro for a term, set up by us.
2. **Community and student radio stations.** They get a Team desk for the station and a callout page for listeners who produce. We get a shared desk that new presenters see on day one. Offer: beta terms, a case study only if they want one.
3. **Distributors and label services.** They get a recommended way for their labels to receive and sort tracks, plus an onboarding page for their roster. We get labels as Team buyers. No data sharing either way.
4. **Music-tech and scene newsletters.** They get a short, honest piece on how receipts work (one signal, one meaning) and a reader code for Pro. We get the reach. No paid placement presented as editorial.
5. **Venues and club nights (London, Lagos, Accra).** They get a callout per night ("send Afro house for {night}") with a deadline, and their residents share one desk. We get a link on a flyer and a story QR in the room.

## 6. Sender/producer advisor

**What senders need to see or hear to trust a link**
1. Who's receiving it: the receiver's name, photo and profile links on the page, and only true claims (email confirmed, no "verified").
2. What happens to the file, before they upload: who hears it, that it isn't published, how long it's kept (90 days undecided, 30 for not for me).
3. Signals with exact meanings: Listened = 30 seconds of real playback. Shown on the receipt, in plain words.
4. No account, no fee, ever: an email code and a receipt link. Say it near the drop box.
5. A brief they can act on (BPM, genres, deadline), so they know a callout is real and current, and the file goes out as the original.

**What would make them avoid it**
1. Any fee, "priority" or boost. Paid feedback reads as a scam.
2. Signals that overclaim or go quiet: a "Listened" that wasn't, or a receipt that never changes with no word on why.
3. Rights grabs or vague terms: any hint the file could be shared, sold or released without asking. DJ-set use must stay opt-in.
